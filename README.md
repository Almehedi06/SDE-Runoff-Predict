# SDE-Runoff-Predict
SDE-based model for runoff prediction using rainfall and temperature data from ~670 catchments across the USA. Captures variability and extremes using probabilistic approaches, offering improvements over deterministic models.

Streamflow dynamics are influenced by system stochasticity - produced by variable precipitation, soil heterogeneity, and other similar natural random processes within the catchment. Traditional deterministic models, such as ODEs, often are unable to capture this variability, resulting in reduced reliability, especially for extreme events like floods. We argue that stochastic differential equations (SDEs) address these limitations by introducing randomness into the modeling equations that describe the dynamics, offering a more realistic and flexible representation of streamflow.

This study demonstrates the effectiveness of SDEs in capturing streamflow variability and extremes, comparing their performance with deterministic models, quantifying uncertainty, and forecasting system response for extreme rainfall events. We share results from our analysis that shed light on the SDE model’s ability to achieve ensemble coverage and improved predictive accuracy across multiple stations where traditional models may fall short.
